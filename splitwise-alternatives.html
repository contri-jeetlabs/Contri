#2. Tricount — Good for Casual Road Trips

Popular in Europe

Tricount (owned by European digital bank bunq) allows users to create a shared expense pool and share a link with friends. It doesn't strictly limit daily entries like Splitwise, making it a viable alternative for holidays.

However, Tricount is monetized with banner ads unless users pay a subscription, and its debt simplification logic is relatively basic compared to Contri's 83% transaction reduction graph.

Verdict: A reliable second choice for European travelers, though compromised by ads and bunq banking promotions.

#3. Splid — Clean Offline Design with In-App Purchases

Offline Capable

Splid offers a clean, minimalistic aesthetic and functions well without an active internet connection. It excels for international vacations where cellular data might be limited.

However, Splid limits users to only 1 active group on the free version. To unlock unlimited groups or synchronize across multiple devices seamlessly, users must purchase an in-app upgrade.

Verdict: Great offline interface, but paywalls limit ongoing multi-group management.

#4. Settle Up — Feature Rich, But Cluttered

Cross-Platform

Settle Up has been around for many years and offers solid currency conversions and multiple payment methods (including PayPal integration).

The downside is the cluttered user experience. The free tier contains frequent banner ads and prompts to upgrade to Settle Up Premium for receipt storage and extra transaction colors.

Verdict: Functional, but the UI feels dated and cluttered compared to Contri's modern zero-ad experience.

#5. Tab — Best for Single Restaurant Bill Itemization

Receipt Scanner

Tab takes a photo of an itemized paper receipt at a restaurant and lets everyone tap the items they ordered, automatically calculating tax and tip proportionally.

While Tab is fantastic for a single meal, it cannot manage ongoing multi-week group balances, roommate rent, or road trips.

Verdict: A single-use restaurant tool, not a full group expense manager.

#6. Plates by Splitwise — Standalone Dining Splitter

Single Meal Only

Plates is a spinoff app by Splitwise specifically for dining out. Like Tab, it splits food courses and tip among up to 10 diners. However, it lacks long-term balance memory and does not connect with roommates or trip budgets.

Verdict: Simple for one dinner, useless for ongoing groups.

#7. Splitwise — The Legacy Incumbent

Crippled Free Tier

Splitwise remains the most recognized name, but its free experience is now one of the worst in the market. The aggressive 3-4 daily expense cap, mandatory 10-second countdown timers, and $39.99/year fee for debt simplification have led to massive user backlash on Reddit and App Store reviews.

Verdict: Only recommended if you are willing to pay $39.99/year for Splitwise Pro.

How to Choose the Right Expense App in 2026

Before moving your friend group to a new app, check these 5 essential criteria:

  1. No Daily Quotas: Ensure the app does not freeze your ability to log expenses after 3 or 4 entries during trips.
  2. Zero Ads & Timers: Waiting 10 seconds per expense quickly ruins group dinners and road trips.
  3. Algorithmic Debt Simplification: Verify that the app resolves circular debts (e.g. A owes B, B owes C) automatically without charging an upgrade fee.
  4. Multi-Payer Support: Real-world purchases often have two or more people contributing cash toward a single bill (e.g., Airbnb deposit).
  5. Privacy First: The app should never demand your online banking credentials or credit card numbers.
